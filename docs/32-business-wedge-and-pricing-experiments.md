# 32 — Business Wedge and Pricing Experiments

## Current stance

Build V1 as a product that can reveal a business. Do not force enterprise requirements into the first experience.

## Most plausible recurring wedge

Documentary, video-essay, adaptation, and biopic researchers have recurring projects, measurable research time, and a reason to pay for better evidence orchestration. Movie enthusiasts remain essential because they test delight and identity, but they may not be the first reliable revenue segment.

## Product positioning

Consumer promise:

> Investigate the worlds behind the films that stay with you.

Creator promise:

> Build a sourced understanding of the world before you write it.

Company thesis:

> The investigation engine, beginning with the world's best Movie Investigator.

Do not lead with “AI script generator,” “RAG for movies,” or “Perplexity for film.”

## Pricing hypotheses to test

### Enthusiast

- limited live investigations per month;
- unlimited saved curated cases;
- notes, playlists, and case-world view;
- lower cost model routing.

### Creator

- higher research budgets;
- source import;
- creator modes;
- dossier and evidence export;
- longer case memory;
- private projects;
- advanced locator and verification runs.

### Team / studio later

- shared projects;
- source permissions;
- reviewer roles;
- audit history;
- organization knowledge;
- contractual privacy controls.

Do not set final prices before measuring cost per activated and retained case. Test willingness to pay using a real constrained pilot, not hypothetical surveys.

## Distribution experiments

1. Publish one exceptional public investigation tied to a culturally active film.
2. Partner with small documentary and video-essay creators, not only famous channels.
3. Offer a research pilot for an upcoming real video.
4. Create sourced “after the credits” mini-trails that link into a full case.
5. Reach film schools, screenwriting groups, adaptation workshops, and history-film communities.
6. Let creators share a read-only case trail with provenance.

## Defensibility hypotheses

The moat is not model access. Possible compounding assets:

- specialist evaluation data;
- verified film-source graph and resolver quality;
- version/cut identity;
- source independence mappings;
- interaction data about useful next leads without storing private content;
- creator workflow integrations;
- trust earned through reliable provenance;
- highly refined investigation sequencing;
- user knowledge graph and cross-case memory, portable and user-controlled.

## Kill criteria

Reconsider the business thesis if, after multiple iterations:

- users praise design but do not start second cases;
- creators only want final scripts rather than the investigation;
- exact-source verification is economically unsustainable;
- generic research tools produce equivalent outcomes with trivial prompting;
- no cohort uses the product at least monthly for real work;
- source access prevents enough useful coverage to deliver the promise.

The project may still remain an exceptional portfolio and consumer experience.
